Motor Insurance Premiums under a Bonus-Malus System

Motor premiums in Indonesia are still mostly flat rates, so a careful driver pays the same as one who claims every year. A Bonus-Malus system is a fairer way to price it: the premium drops after claim-free years and rises after claims. I modelled claim frequency as Poisson with an exponential parameter, which gives a geometric distribution, and claim severity as exponential with an inverse gamma parameter, which gives a Lomax distribution.

Data
14,505policyholders
89.4% made no claim in the year
Geometric · claim frequency
θ = 8.3506χ² = 1.579, below the 7.815 critical value at α = 5%
Lomax · claim severity
s = 2.4824shape parameter
m = 3,053.38scale parameter

The hat marks an estimate from the data. Together they give a starting premium of $246.66.

Try it: how the premium moves

The table is the premium for the next year after t years with K claims in total. Add years below and the highlighter follows you through the table.

    What the thesis found

    1. A geometric model fits the claim counts. With θ = 8.3506, the Chi-square statistic was 1.579 against a critical value of 7.815 (df = 3, α = 5%), so the model was not rejected.
    2. Counting claim size makes the malus fairer. Three claims in the first year raise the premium by 257.22% when only frequency counts (Table 4.6), but by 76.17% when claim severity is included (Table 4.5).
    3. Larger claims raise the malus; the no-claim bonus does not change. A higher total severity T raises the malus in every K column of Tables 4.7 to 4.9, while the bonus for no claims stays the same: 26.43% after three claim-free years.
    4. The parameters pull bonus and malus in different directions. A higher θ lowers the bonus and raises the malus. A higher scale m lowers the malus, and a higher shape s raises it; neither changes the bonus.

    July and August lose the most: $1.22M a year, 31% of the yearly loss. Rooms cost the most in those months (ADR $127 in July, $140 in August), so each canceled night loses more.

    2 · The earlier the booking, the more likely it cancels

    Cancellation rate by lead time, the days between booking and arrival. In brackets: that group's share of all bookings.

    0 to 30 days (32.3%)18.5%
    31 to 90 days (24.9%)37.6%
    91 to 180 days (22.2%)44.7%
    181 to 365 days (17.9%)55.2%
    More than 365 days (2.7%)67.4%

    Bookings made more than 180 days ahead are 20.6% of all bookings, and 55 to 67% of them cancel. Bookings made within a month cancel at 18.5%.

    3 · Guest behaviour linked to cancelling

    Cancellation rate for guests with and without each behaviour.

    Linked to more cancellations

    Canceled before91.3%
    Never canceled33.9%
    Waited on the waiting list63.6%
    Confirmed right away36.1%

    Linked to fewer cancellations

    Made a special request21.7%
    No special request47.6%
    Changed the booking15.9%
    No change40.7%
    Stayed before10.4%
    First stay37.8%

    A guest who canceled before cancels again 91.3% of the time. Guests who made at least one special request cancel at 21.7%, less than half the rate of guests who made none. These are associations: a guest who already plans to come is more likely to ask for something.

    What I would recommend

    1. Ask long-lead bookings to reconfirmFor bookings made more than 180 days ahead, ask for reconfirmation closer to arrival and a partial prepayment. They are 20.6% of bookings and cancel at 55 to 67%.
    2. Get guests to plan their stay before arrivalInvite special requests and online check-in. Guests with a request cancel at 21.7%, against 47.6% without. Test it first, since the link may not be causal.
    3. Protect July and AugustTighten cancellation terms for these months and review Group bookings, which cancel at 60.7%. July and August make up 31% of the yearly loss.

    What I took from it

    • Compare months per year, not in total. July and August appear 3 times in the data and the other months only twice, so their totals look bigger than they are.
    • A difference is not a cause. Guests with a special request cancel less, but that does not prove the request is what keeps them from canceling.
